# Flow and mental-readiness interventions for athletes: evidence, doses, effect sizes, acute vs. trained (for the "Flow of Ace" pre-match app)

> **Research status note (2026-11-23 pass).** This version replaces the earlier partial notes. It keeps the sound findings from that pass and adds about 27 new searches.
> - Full-text fetching was blocked for pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, nature.com, excli.de and boris.unibe.ch (they are not on the session's network allowlist). Most figures therefore come from abstracts and indexed snippets of the linked pages.
> - A figure that comes only from a snippet is marked "(snippet)".
> - Figures that could not be confirmed are listed under Gaps rather than estimated.

**Evidence grade legend**
- **Strong:** meta-analysis of controlled or randomized trials with consistent results.
- **Mixed:** controlled or meta-analytic support with important caveats, such as heterogeneity, results that fail sensitivity analyses, only subjective outcomes, or low GRADE ratings.
- **Weak / popular:** single-case or small studies, one lab, lab tasks only with no sport replication, or practitioner claims.

**Acute vs. trained legend**
- **ACUTE:** shown to work in a single session or from instructions given minutes before the task.
- **TRAINED:** evidence comes from multi-week programs.

## 1. Mindfulness programs (MAC, MSPE, Aherne 2011, Scott-Hamilton 2016, Bühlmayer 2017, Noetel 2019, 2020s meta-analyses) and flow

### Takeaway
Mindfulness has the largest flow-specific evidence base and the largest pooled flow effect: SMD ≈ 0.9 for flow in Si et al. 2024, and "large" effects on flow in Noetel et al.'s review, though their GRADE rating was low. Every program behind these numbers ran for 4–8 weeks. No athlete study found here shows that a single acute mindfulness session raises measured flow.

Grades:
- Flow and anxiety: **Mixed-to-moderate**.
- Objective performance: **Mixed/weak**. The pooled performance effect is not robust once non-RCTs and subjective outcomes are excluded.

Timing: **TRAINED**.

### Cited Findings
- **Si, Yang & Feng (2024, Frontiers in Psychology)** pooled 11 articles (23 effect sizes, 582 athletes).
  - Flow / "fluency": SMD = 0.92, 95% CI [0.40, 1.43].
  - Mindfulness: SMD = 1.08.
  - Anxiety: SMD = −0.87.
  - Sources: [Frontiers](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1375608/full); [PMC11210447](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11210447/)
- **Noetel, Ciarrochi, Van Zanden & Lonsdale** (Int Rev Sport Exerc Psychol, online 2017, print 2019, 12(1):139–175) reviewed 66 studies with 3,908 participants.
  - Against no-treatment controls in randomized trials: "large effect sizes were found for improving mindfulness, flow, and performance, and lower competitive anxiety."
  - No included study had a low risk of bias, and GRADE quality was **low**.
  - Sources: [Informa abstract](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/1750984X.2017.1387803); [UTS record](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/138664)
- **Aherne, Moran & Lonsdale (2011, The Sport Psychologist 25(2):177–189)** randomized 13 athletes.
  - The 6 who completed a **6-week CD-guided mindfulness program** reported greater flow than before the program and than controls.
  - Performance was not measured. This is described in secondary citations; the full text was not accessed.
  - Sources: [Western Sydney record](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/the-effect-of-mindfulness-training-on-athletes-flow-an-initial-in/); [ACU record](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/87972/the-effect-of-mindfulness-training-on-athletes-flow-an-initial-investigation)
- **Scott-Hamilton, Schutte & Brown (2016, Applied Psychology: Health and Well-Being 8(1):85–103)** ran an **8-week** mindfulness program that included mindful spin-bike training.
  - 27 intervention cyclists vs. 20 wait-list controls. Analyses of covariance showed significant gains in mindfulness and flow and reductions in pessimism.
  - Changes in mindfulness were positively associated with changes in flow.
  - Source: [UNE record](https://rune.une.edu.au/web/handle/1959.11/18914)
- **MSPE, Kaufman, Glass & Arnkoff (2009)**, "Evaluation of MSPE: A new approach to promote flow in athletes," J Clin Sport Psychol 3:334–356.
  - Later papers cite it as linking MSPE to better flow, self-confidence and competitive anxiety in archers. Exact effect sizes were not retrieved.
  - The current book version is a **six-session program**.
  - Sources: [Cambridge chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/mindfulness-and-performance/mindful-sport-performance-enhancement-mspe-development-and-applications/4B13D7499634B1D801F7109760089CF7); [APA book](https://www.apa.org/pubs/books/4317476)
- **MAC (Gardner & Moore protocol) vs. PST, randomized trials:**
  - Gross et al. 2018: 18 NCAA D-III female athletes. MAC reduced substance use, hostility and emotion dysregulation, and coaches rated performance better.
  - Josefsson et al. 2019: 69 elite athletes. MAC produced larger gains than PST in athletic mindfulness, emotion regulation and self-rated performance, with effects mediated by mindfulness and emotion regulation.
  - Outcomes are mostly self-rated or coach-rated.
  - Sources: [Springer, Mindfulness 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12671-019-01098-7); [Kean record](https://researchers.kean.edu/en/publications/an-empirical-examination-comparing-the-mindfulness-acceptance-com/)
- **Bühlmayer et al. (2017, Sports Medicine)** reported a pooled SMD of 3.62 on performance-relevant parameters, with I² = 98%.
  - The outcomes were mostly mindfulness scores, not flow, so the estimate is unreliable as a flow figure.
  - Source: [PubMed 28664327](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28664327/)
- **2023 RCT meta-analysis** (cited as Wang et al. in the earlier pass; authorship not re-verified) of mindfulness-based interventions on athletic performance and related factors. Flow is among the outcomes, but no flow effect size was retrieved.
  - Source: [PMC9915077](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9915077/)
- **2024 Sports Medicine meta-analysis** of psychological interventions on athletic performance.
  - Mindfulness/acceptance, PST and imagery each had significant moderate effects.
  - These effects were no longer significant once non-randomized trials and subjective performance outcomes were removed.
  - Source: [PubMed 37812334](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37812334/)
- **"Brief" mindfulness, Liu et al.**: 57 student-athletes randomized to brief mindfulness training (n = 29) or control (n = 28).
  - Flow and resilience both improved, and resilience partly mediated the flow gain.
  - This was a multi-session pre/post design, not a single session.
  - Source: [PMC8166472](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8166472/)
- **Single-session evidence:**
  - A registered trial tested a single **30-min** mindfulness induction vs. relaxation vs. control in 35 athletes. Its outcomes are anxiety, affect and brain activation, not flow. ([ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05452629](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05452629))
  - A 15-min audio mindfulness task with flow as an outcome was tested only in sedentary adults. ([Lancaster eprint](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/147727/1/PMS_20_0033.R1.jdb.pdf), snippet)

### Inferences
- The flow effect of about SMD 0.9 rests on fewer than 600 athletes, self-reported flow and unblinded designs. Treat it as an upper bound.
- In the app, mindfulness fits as a **4–8-week off-court program** with roughly weekly sessions plus daily practice. A short pre-match breath or attention anchor can be justified as a cue for a skill already trained, but it has not been tested on its own for flow.
- MAC/MSPE-style acceptance messaging fits a pre-match reminder, e.g. "nerves are allowed; attend to the next ball." Its acute effect is untested.

### Gaps
- Exact flow effect sizes for Kaufman 2009 MSPE and for Aherne 2011; full texts were not accessed.
- Si et al. 2024 moderators (session length, program weeks) were not extracted; PMC was blocked.
- No athlete RCT was found with an **acute single-session** mindfulness manipulation and flow (FSS-2 / DFS-2 / short flow scales) as the outcome.

## 2. Overall flow-intervention evidence and psychological skills training (PST) packages

### Takeaway
Psychological skills training packages improve sport performance moderately, with g ≈ 0.5–0.6 against controls. For flow itself, reviews conclude that interventions raise some flow dimensions, but none conclusively shows that an intervention *induces* flow.

Grades:
- "PST improves performance": **Mixed-to-strong**.
- "An app can reliably induce flow": **Weak**.

### Cited Findings
- **Brown & Fletcher (2017, Sports Medicine 47:77–99)**: psychological and psychosocial interventions vs. no-treatment or placebo controls.
  - Hedges' g = 0.57, 95% CI [0.22, 0.92], k = 35, n = 997.
  - The effect "may last at least a month" after the intervention ends.
  - Larger effects for psychosocial and coach-delivered interventions.
  - Sources: [Springer](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s40279-016-0552-7); [Bath record](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/effects-of-psychological-and-psychosocial-interventions-on-sport-/)
- **Meta-analysis of sport-psychology meta-analyses**: moderate beneficial effect, d = 0.51, across variables hypothesized to enhance performance.
  - Source: [PMC8849618](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8849618)
- **2023 systematic review of flow interventions** in sport and exercise (Int Rev Sport Exerc Psychol), from a snippet; authorship not confirmed.
  - It found little evidence that flow interventions are effective.
  - The most common approaches were mindfulness (30.03%), hypnosis (17.24%) and imagery (13.79%).
  - Interventions improved some flow dimensions, but no study conclusively showed flow being induced.
  - Source: [SCU record](https://researchportal.scu.edu.au/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/A-systematic-review-of-flow-interventions/991012942199702368)
- **2017 review of flow training in elite athletes**: 4 studies showed significant or sizeable increases in flow, and all reported performance gains.
  - Source: [GJSEPER 2017 PDF](https://www2.worc.ac.uk/gjseper/documents/Flow_states_and_performance_in_elite_athletes_6_16-28.pdf)

### Inferences
- App copy should promise "build the conditions for flow": challenge–skill balance, clear goals, focus, control and confidence. It should not promise "get into the zone."

### Gaps
- Per-intervention pooled flow effect sizes from the 2023 flow review are unverified.

## 3. Specific techniques: imagery (PETTLEP), hypnosis, self-talk, goal setting, music, HRV biofeedback

### Takeaway
- **Self-talk:** ES = 0.48 for performance. **Strong**. Best when trained, and short cue words are usable in a match.
- **Imagery:** d ≈ 0.43; more training intensity gives larger effects. **Strong for performance after sensitivity caveats, Mixed for flow**. Mostly **TRAINED**.
- **Process goals:** d = 1.36 for performance, vs. outcome goals d = 0.09. **Moderate-to-strong**. Setting them takes minutes, so pre-match use is feasible.
- **Hypnosis and music for flow:** single-case designs, mostly one lab group. **Weak-to-mixed**.
- **Music:** small general performance and affect effects. **Strong for affect, small for performance**. **ACUTE**.
- **HRV biofeedback:** promising but thin. **Weak/mixed**. **TRAINED**.

### Cited Findings

**Self-talk**
- **Hatzigeorgiadis et al. (2011, Perspectives on Psychological Science)**: 32 studies, 62 effect sizes.
  - Overall ES = 0.48, 95% CI [0.38, 0.58].
  - Larger effects for fine vs. gross tasks and for novel vs. well-learned tasks.
  - Instructional self-talk is better for fine tasks.
  - Interventions with prior self-talk **training** were more effective.
  - Sources: [APS](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/perspectives/1745691611413136/); [UTH](https://ir.lib.uth.gr/xmlui/handle/11615/28461)
- **Bayesian reanalysis** (Corcoran & Steele, preprint, not peer reviewed): the overall effect is still positive, but some moderators are weaker.
  - Source: [SportRxiv 348](https://sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/348/version/450)
- **Hatzigeorgiadis, Galanis, Zourbanos & Theodorakis (2014, J Appl Sport Psychol)**: a **10-week** self-talk program in 41 young swimmers.
  - The intervention group improved competitive performance more than controls, measured across 2 competitions 10 weeks apart.
  - Source: [UTH PDF](https://eclass.uth.gr/modules/document/file.php/PE_P_160/self_talk/4.3.22Hatzigeorgiadis_etaL_2014.pdf)

**Imagery**
- **Simonsmeier et al. (2020/21, Int Rev Sport Exerc Psychol)**: imagery interventions improved motor performance, motivational and affective outcomes.
  - Overall d = 0.431, 95% CI [0.298, 0.563].
  - More intense imagery training was associated with larger effects.
  - Source: [Bielefeld record](https://pub.uni-bielefeld.de/record/2943784)
- **2025 multilevel meta-analysis (Behavioral Sciences)**: 86 studies, 3,593 athletes.
  - The best dose was **about 10 minutes, 3 times per week, over about 100 days**.
  - The authors caution that larger studies are needed.
  - Sources: [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328X/15/5/685/html); [PMC12109254](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12109254/) (snippet)
- **2024 meta-analysis on tennis serve**: the effect of motor imagery training on serve performance was "questionable."
  - Source: [via search, MDPI Behav Sci 14(3):207](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-328X/14/3/207) (snippet)
- **Koehn, Morris & Watt (2014, The Sport Psychologist 28:48–59)**: tailored imagery for flow in tennis. Single-case multiple-baseline design, N = 4 nationally ranked players.
  - The scripts targeted challenge–skill balance, clear goals, concentration and control.
  - 3 of 4 showed sustained increases in flow. All 4 improved serve, groundstroke and ranking.
  - Source: [Hope repository](https://hira.hope.ac.uk/id/eprint/1576/); [VU record](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/29928/)
- **2024 Sports Medicine meta-analysis**: imagery effects were moderate but not robust to excluding non-RCTs and subjective outcomes.
  - Source: [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-023-01931-z)

**Hypnosis (Pates and colleagues)**
- **Basketball**: 5 collegiate players. All increased three-point performance and flow.
  - Sources: [Derby PDF](https://repository.derby.ac.uk/download/b7cb000fbf725c443cfe57f85746988589333dbe207600b8acc1ca8c44da53fc/63316/Basketball.pdf); [Hartpury](https://pure.hartpury.ac.uk/en/publications/the-effect-of-a-hypnosis-intervention-on-performance-and-flow-sta/)
- **Golf putting**: 5 golfers. All improved putting and flow, with no overlapping data points.
  - Source: [PubMed 11219648](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11219648/)
- **Golf chipping**: ABA design, N = 3. Performance improved, and 2 of 3 returned to baseline after withdrawal.
  - Source: [Coaching Science Abstracts](https://coachsci.sdsu.edu/csa/vol94/pates2.htm)
- **Evidence base**: a 2019 review found 17 hypnosis-in-sport articles (6 controlled, 11 single-case), mostly from Pates, Maynard and colleagues.
  - Source: [CORE PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/287121544.pdf)

**Goal setting**
- **Williamson et al. (2022, Int Rev Sport Exerc Psychol)**: 27 studies.
  - Performance effects: **process goals d = 1.36**, performance goals d = 0.44, **outcome goals d = 0.09**.
  - Specific (d = 0.37) and non-specific (d = 0.72) goals did not differ significantly.
  - Process goals and self-efficacy: d = 1.11.
  - The authors say conclusions on psychological outcomes are rarely possible.
  - Source: [T&F open access](https://tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1750984X.2022.2116723)

**Music**
- **Terry, Karageorghis et al. (2020, Psychological Bulletin 146(2):91–117)**: 139 studies, 598 effect sizes, 3,599 participants.
  - Affective valence: g = 0.48.
  - Physical performance: g = 0.31 [0.25, 0.36].
  - Heart rate: g = 0.07, not significant.
  - Effects were larger in exercise than in sport, and larger with fast tempo.
  - Sources: [USQ record](https://research.usq.edu.au/item/q57x6/effects-of-music-in-exercise-and-sport-a-meta-analytic-review); [Brunel AAM](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/19875)
- **Pates, Karageorghis, Fryer & Maynard (2003, Psych Sport Exerc 4:415–427)**: self-selected pre-task music. Single-case design, 3 netball players.
  - 2 of 3 reported more flow, and all 3 improved shooting.
  - Sources: [Derby](https://repository.derby.ac.uk/item/94619/effects-of-asynchronous-music-on-flow-states-and-shooting-performance-among-netball-players); [CORE PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/29140861.pdf)

**HRV biofeedback**
- **2021 preliminary meta-analysis**: 4 studies, 115 athletes. It examined HRV and respiration responses only, not performance.
  - Source: [PubMed 34187114](https://www.pubmed.gov/34187114/) (snippet)
- **Systematic review of RCTs**: 6 studies, 187 athletes. HRV biofeedback *may* improve fine and gross motor function.
  - Source: [PMC7386140](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7386140) (snippet)
- **2025 Bayesian meta-analysis (Frontiers)**: 41 RCTs of biofeedback and neurofeedback. Large pooled effect on athletic performance, but heterogeneity was high, samples were small and the authors call for more rigorous trials.
  - Source: [Frontiers fpsyg.2025.1662868](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1662868/full) (snippet)

### Inferences
- Self-talk works better for fine and novel tasks and needs prior training. For volleyball, the likely biggest payoff is on the serve and on precision skills, with cue words practiced in training and then used in matches.
- Process goals are the cheapest high-yield pre-match item: 1–3 controllable process goals such as "high toss, firm wrist" or "call the ball early." Note that the d = 1.36 comes from relatively few studies, and the review does not report how long goals must be in place before they work.
- Self-selected music is a reasonable warm-up mood and arousal tool: small but robust effects on affect and performance. Its link to flow rests on a 3-person study.
- A hypnosis-style audio script may lose its effect when practice stops. The app would need ongoing reinforcement.

### Gaps
- PETTLEP-specific effect sizes vs. traditional imagery were not retrieved, nor were Holmes & Collins 2001 trials.
- Exact Bayesian effect size and moderators for HRV biofeedback (2025). Full text not accessed.
- The goal-setting meta-analysis does not specify how long before a performance process goals need to be set.

## 4. Setting the mindset / mental readiness: reappraisal, challenge vs. threat, implementation intentions, anxiety direction, confidence

### Takeaway
This category has the best evidence for **acute, minutes-before** delivery.
- **Arousal / stress reappraisal** ("this is excitement / arousal helps me"): pooled d = 0.23 across RCTs (Bosshard & Gomez 2024), and d = 0.34 on public performance tasks. Grade: **Strong evidence of a small effect**, mostly from lab tasks.
- **Challenge-state instructions** given just before a task improved golf putting in novices and experienced golfers (Moore et al. 2012, 2013). Grade: **Mixed-to-strong**, from a consistent lab line and 2019/2025 reviews.
- **Implementation intentions**: d = 0.65 in general (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006), with a tennis application (Achtziger et al. 2008). Grade: **Moderate**. The plans can be formed in minutes.
- **Confidence / self-efficacy**: correlates with performance (r ≈ 0.31–0.38). Direct trials of "highlight review" interventions were not found. Grade: **Mixed** (correlational).

### Cited Findings

**Stress and arousal reappraisal**
- **Brooks (2014, J Exp Psychol: General 143(3):1144–1158)**: a short cue ("I am excited" said aloud, or a "get excited" message) vs. "calm down."
  - Reappraisal raised felt excitement and improved karaoke singing, public speaking and math performance.
  - Most people believe calming down is best.
  - Sources: [PubMed 24364682](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24364682/); [APA press release](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2013/12/performance-anxiety.aspx)
- **Jamieson et al.**: arousal-reappraisal instructions improved GRE quantitative performance vs. no-instruction controls, in the lab and in a follow-up.
  - Source: [Harvard DASH PDF](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037e-3a25-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content) (snippet)
- **Bosshard & Gomez (2024, Scientific Reports 14:7923)**: meta-analysis of RCTs of stress arousal reappraisal (SAR) and stress-is-enhancing (SIE) mindset interventions on task performance. 44 effect sizes.
  - Overall **d = 0.23** (p < 0.001).
  - Mixed interventions (k = 5): d = 0.45. SAR-only (k = 33): d = 0.22.
  - Public performance tasks (k = 14): **d = 0.34**. Cognitive written tasks (k = 30): d = 0.20.
  - The authors call these interventions "not silver bullets" but a low-cost, low-threshold approach.
  - Sources: [PMC10994935](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10994935/); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/23599c9fe178423e8de691ad05ed9cd8) (snippet)

**Challenge vs. threat (TCTSA)**
- **Jones, Meijen, McCarthy & Sheffield (2009) TCTSA** (Int Rev Sport Exerc Psychol 2:161–180).
  - Self-efficacy, perceived control and approach achievement goals produce a challenge state.
  - In a challenge state, emotions are perceived as helpful.
  - The state affects effort, attention, decision-making and physical functioning.
  - The revised TCTSA-R (Meijen et al. 2020) describes four states.
  - Sources: [St Mary's record](https://research.stmarys.ac.uk/id/eprint/1926); [Kent PDF](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/id/document/15982); [Frontiers 2020 TCTSA-R](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00126/pdf)
- **Moore, Vine, Wilson & Freeman (2012, Psychophysiology)**: 127 novices. Challenge or threat states were manipulated **via task instructions** immediately before putting.
  - The challenge group putted more accurately, reported more favorable emotions, and showed more effective gaze (quiet eye), kinematics and muscle activity.
  - Kinematics mediated the performance effect.
  - Source: [PMC3677799](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3677799)
- **Moore, Wilson, Vine, Coussens & Freeman (2013, JSEP), "Champ or chump?"**: experienced golfers randomized to challenge or threat instructions before a competitive putting task. The manipulation worked and the challenge group outperformed the threat group.
  - Source: [Glos eprint](https://eprints.glos.ac.uk/2878/1/Champ%20or%20Chump.pdf)
- **Hase, O'Brien, Moore & Freeman (2019, Sport Exerc Perform Psychol)**: 38 studies supporting performance benefits of a challenge state, with large variation in measures and tasks.
  - Source: [Bath record](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/the-relationship-between-challenge-and-threat-states-and-performa/)
- **Hase et al. (2025, EXCLI J 24:151–176)** updated meta-analysis (2017–2024 studies): challenge states outperform threat states across domains including sport. Effects were "small" and risk of bias generally low.
  - Snippets disagree on study counts: one says 47 studies / N = 5,483, another 62 / N = 7,418.
  - Source: [PMC11869992](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11869992/) (snippet)

**Implementation intentions**
- **Achtziger, Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2008, PSPB 34:381–393)**: if-then plans shield goal striving from unwanted thoughts and feelings. A tennis study within the paper showed the plans helped players manage disruptive cognitive, motivational and physiological states during matches.
  - Sources: [PubMed 18272806](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18272806/); [Konstanz PDF](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/sites/default/files/08_Achtziger_Gollwitzer_Sheeran_Implem.intentions_and_sh%282%29.pdf)
- **Gollwitzer & Sheeran (2006) meta-analysis** (general, not sport-specific): d = 0.65 across 94 studies and more than 8,000 participants.
  - Source: [Konstanz KOPS](https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/server/api/core/bitstreams/eb48c8cf-7855-478e-90ec-1ea6d44f8252/content)

**Anxiety interpretation (direction vs. intensity, Jones & Hanton)**
- Swimmers with positive goal expectancies rated their anxiety as more facilitative at the same intensity.
- Elite performers interpreted anxiety as more facilitative than non-elites.
- These come from primary studies; no pooled estimate was found.
- Sources: [Coaching Science Abstracts](https://coachsci.sdsu.edu/csa/vol94/jones.htm); [Mellalieu et al. 2008 review PDF](https://eclass.uth.gr/modules/document/file.php/PE_P_159/key%20readings/anxiety_Mellalieu%20et%20al%202008.pdf)

**Confidence and self-efficacy**
- **Moritz et al. 2000**: mean self-efficacy–performance r = 0.38 (45 studies).
- **Lochbaum et al. 2023** (pre-event self-efficacy): r = 0.31 [0.22, 0.40], with wide prediction intervals.
- Source: [PMC10675036](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10675036/)
- **2003 meta-analysis** (k = 48): self-confidence r = 0.24 with performance; cognitive anxiety r = −0.10.
  - Source: [PubMed 12846532](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12846532/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended pre-match "mindset set" sequence (about 2–4 minutes).** This is assembled from separately tested pieces; the combined package is untested:
  1. **Reappraisal.** Example: "Racing heart = my body getting ready. Say: *I'm excited*."
  2. **Challenge framing** that emphasizes resources and control (TCTSA: efficacy, control, approach goals).
  3. **1–3 process goals.**
  4. **2–3 if-then plans** for adversity. Example: "If I miss a serve, then I exhale, say 'next ball', and pick my target."
  5. **Brief confidence recall** of past successes. Bandura's mastery source; the specific highlight-review intervention is untested.
- The Moore et al. instruction manipulations were delivered in minutes just before performance. That is the strongest direct support for an app delivering mindset framing acutely.
- The reappraisal effect is small (d ≈ 0.2–0.35). Expect a modest edge, not a transformation. The mixed-intervention subgroup (d = 0.45, k = 5) suggests that combining reappraisal with other content may help.

### Gaps
- No sport-specific RCT of "I'm excited" reappraisal on match performance was found. The evidence is mostly from lab tasks.
- Hase et al. 2025 pooled effect size: snippets give conflicting study counts, and PMC/EXCLI full text was blocked.
- Effect sizes and sample details for the Achtziger 2008 tennis study were not retrieved.
- No controlled trials of gratitude or values reminders, or athlete-identity and autonomy priming, on pre-competition readiness were searched or found. Treat these as **unevidenced** for this purpose.
- No meta-analysis of directional anxiety interpretation found.

## 5. Pre-performance and between-point / in-match reset routines

### Takeaway
Pre-performance routines (PPRs) reliably improve performance, including under pressure.
- Pre-post designs: SMC = 0.31.
- Experimental designs: moderate-to-large effects.
- Effects did not depend on skill level or routine type.

Short stand-alone routines such as **quiet eye** (QE training, d = 0.84 for performance) and **left-hand dynamic handgrip** work and fit the seconds available between points.

Grade: **Strong for performance**. The direct link to flow was not studied. Peer-reviewed evidence specific to between-point routines in tennis or volleyball was not found; that material is mostly practitioner advice (**popular**).

### Cited Findings
- **Rupprecht, Tran & Gröpel** (Int Rev Sport Exerc Psychol, online 2021; 17:39–64): 112 effect sizes.
  - Pre-post: SMC = 0.31 [0.18, 0.44].
  - Experimental designs: moderate-to-large effects under both low and high pressure.
  - No moderation by PPR type, age, gender or skill level.
  - Stand-alone PPRs (quiet eye, left-hand dynamic handgrip) were effective.
  - Source: [T&F abstract](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/1750984X.2021.1944271)
- **Lebeau et al. (2016, JSEP)**, quiet eye meta-analysis. The intervention synthesis (9 articles) found QE training increased QE duration (d = 1.53) and performance (**d = 0.84**).
  - Sources: [PubMed 27633956](https://www.pubmed.gov/27633956/); [Lund record](https://lup.lub.lu.se/search/publication/6ef0330b-9a48-4cc0-b8ea-83f98afc5b5a)
- **Cotterill (2010, Int Rev Sport Exerc Psychol 3:132–153)**: understanding of routines "falls somewhat short" of what is needed to confidently prescribe them. Their function, content and teaching methods remain open questions.
  - Source: [CORE PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/288358786.pdf) (snippet)
- **Basketball free throws:**
  - Lonsdale & Tam (2008, J Sports Sci 26:259–266) studied 284 NBA playoff free throws for temporal vs. behavioral routine consistency. The results section was not retrieved.
  - Gooding & Gardner (2009), Division I: routine length and consistency did not predict free-throw success.
  - Sources: [PubMed 17943592](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17943592/); [Gooding & Gardner PDF](https://eclass.uth.gr/modules/document/file.php/PE_P_160/3rd%20presentation/Gooding%20and%20Gardner%20%282009%29.pdf)
- **Tennis between-point routines:** the frameworks found are coaching material, not tested protocols.
  - USTA "green / yellow" routines. ([USTA](https://www.usta.com/en/home/improve/tips-and-instruction/national/green-light--yellow-light--routines-to-succeed-on-the-tennis-cou.html))
  - A 4-step model: react, reset, plan, pre-serve. ([coaching blog](https://karolina-sznycer.squarespace.com/blog/2020/4/20/4-steps-of-between-point-routines))
- **Press coverage of the Rupprecht meta-analysis**: about 15 sports and about 800 athletes, benefits for novices and elites.
  - Source: [Univ. Vienna press release](https://medienportal.univie.ac.at/media/aktuelle-pressemeldungen/detailansicht/artikel/athletes-with-a-pre-performance-routine-perform-better)

### Inferences
- A volleyball serve routine (exhale, cue word, target image or quiet-eye fixation on the target, consistent toss) fits the PPR and QE evidence. It can be trained in the app and executed in about 5–10 s. The timing is an inference.
- A "reset after error" routine (physical release, then a self-talk cue, then a refocus on the next ball's process goal) combines PPR, self-talk and implementation-intention evidence. It has not been tested as a package.
- Because routine type did not moderate effects, players can personalize routines.

### Gaps
- Lonsdale & Tam 2008 results; full text not accessed.
- No peer-reviewed controlled study of between-point routines in tennis or volleyball was found.

## 6. Volleyball-specific mental skills interventions

### Takeaway
The volleyball evidence is small and mostly non-randomized: team case studies, correlational season data and quasi-experiments with novices. It points the same direction as the general literature: imagery, self-talk and a serve routine are associated with better serving. Grade: **Weak-to-mixed** for volleyball; rely on the general meta-analyses.

### Cited Findings
- **Shoenfelt & Griffith (2008, Perceptual and Motor Skills 107:293–306)**: a mental skills program for serving in an NCAA team (N = 11) with a 3-phase serve routine.
  - End-of-season imagery use and serve-routine use correlated significantly with good-serve percentage.
  - Serve self-efficacy rose over the season.
  - Sources: [PubMed 18986056](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18986056/); [WKU program description](https://people.wku.edu/betsy.shoenfelt/vbmentalskillsserveroutine.htm)
- **WKU thesis (2005)**: D-I team (N = 12) trained in relaxation, imagery and attention. Imagery use correlated r = .82 with good-serve percentage (correlational).
  - Source: [WKU TopSCHOLAR](https://digitalcommons.wku.edu/theses/484)
- **Nazari (2024, J Motor Control Learn)**: novice adolescent girls, **24 sessions**.
  - Imagery, self-talk and the combination each improved serve performance and learning, including at 2-week retention. The combination was best.
  - Randomization was not confirmed.
  - Source: [Brieflands](https://brieflands.com/journals/jmcl/articles/144891)
- **Indonesian jump-serve imagery study**: one group, 12 athletes, about 4% improvement. Weak design.
  - Source: [UNNES PDF](https://journal.unnes.ac.id/journals/peshr/article/download/27789/4768/91046)

### Inferences
- The app's volleyball content should draw on the general meta-analyses and treat the serve as the main closed-skill target for routines, self-talk and imagery.

### Gaps
- No volleyball RCT measuring flow was found.
- No volleyball study of acute pre-match mindset manipulations (reappraisal, challenge instructions) was found.

## 7. Acute (single-session) vs. multi-week: doses and app delivery tiers

### Takeaway
Acute, pre-match deliverable with controlled evidence:
- Arousal reappraisal
- Challenge-framed instructions
- Implementation intentions
- Process goals
- Self-selected music
- Stand-alone PPR elements, such as quiet eye cues and the handgrip

Needs weeks of training:
- Mindfulness (4–8 weeks), which has the biggest flow effects
- Imagery (about 10 min, 3 times per week, for about 100 days, as the best dose)
- Self-talk training (about 10 weeks in competitive studies)
- QE training
- HRV biofeedback
- Hypnosis

### Inferences
- **App structure suggested by the evidence (inference):**
  - **Weekly program (off-court):** mindfulness (6–8 wk), imagery of serve and passing (about 10 min, 3 times per week), and self-talk cue training.
  - **Pre-match (3–5 min, acute):** reappraisal, challenge framing, process goals, if-then adversity plans, an optional music playlist, and a brief imagery run-through of cues already trained.
  - **In-match (seconds):** serve PPR with a quiet-eye target cue, and an error-reset routine with a trained cue word.
- Measure outcomes in-app with a short flow scale, and use serve percentage as an objective outcome, so the app's own effect can be validated.

### Gaps
- No athlete study was found where a single acute session of any intervention raised a validated flow scale (FSS-2/DFS-2) against a control. The closest are single-case music and imagery studies across sessions, and the acute challenge-instruction studies, which measured performance and emotion rather than flow.
