# Verification: arousal, breathing, music, focus and vision figures

Checked 2026-11-23. Method: PMC full text was read through the NCBI BioC API (`www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/research/bionlp/RESTful/pmcoa.cgi/BioC_json/<PMCID>`), PubMed abstracts through the same API's `pubmed.cgi` endpoint, and Frontiers HTML with curl. The normal PMC and PubMed pages, CORE, Springer and Taylor & Francis returned bot challenges (reCAPTCHA or Cloudflare). Several papers are paywalled (J Sports Sci, JESP, JSEP, Psych Bull, Neurosci Biobehav Rev), so for those only the PubMed abstract was checked. The level of source is stated for every row.

Status key: VERIFIED = the figure matches the source. CORRECTED = the figure or the link is wrong, and the correct value is given. UNVERIFIABLE = the source could not be read.

## Notes

- **Item 10 link error.** The app's "Keep screen time short" card (`src/app.html` and `index.html`, line ~1229/1235, also in `flow-of-ace/src/app.html`), the report (`reports/Flow state and eye hand coordination.md` line 41) and `eye_hand_coordination.md` line 205 all link https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34000894/. That PMID is a resistance-training study, so replace it with https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34404292/. The claim itself ("30 min social media impaired volleyball decision-making in small studies") is supported by PMID 34404292 (n = 24) and PMC11845908 (n = 14).
- **Item 14 wording.** In the app's "Screen colors" card, "A dark screen caused somewhat less eye fatigue than a light one" goes beyond this study. Its *visual fatigue* score did not differ between modes. Only CFF and dry-eye symptoms differed, and the "may help reduce" conclusion is the authors' interpretation. The study also does not support "mostly in bright rooms", because ambient light was fixed at 300–500 lux. Suggested wording: "In a small study (30 users, 1 h), dark and light mode caused similar visual fatigue, with slightly better dry-eye and flicker-fusion scores in dark mode."
- **Item 2.** If the app says "about half a standard deviation", the source supports it ("almost one-half"). The exact value to cite is d = 0.44.
- **Item 1.** The full text is needed to name the HRV band. Any app or report text that says "LF/HF" or "HF" specifically should be treated as unverified until the paper is read.
- **Item 6.** HRV did not change differently between groups, and resting HR did not either. The breathwork advantage over meditation applied to positive affect and respiratory rate. State anxiety fell in every group, with no difference between groups.
