## 1. Market size: volleyball players, and the sports-psychology / mental-fitness app market

### Takeaway
Volleyball has a very large casual base: the FIVB's figure of about 800 million is marketing language and is often framed as "fans". The organized, reachable base is much smaller. It is roughly 0.5M paying USA Volleyball members, about 0.5M US high-school girls, about 20k NCAA indoor players, and about 58k registered members in the Czech federation. No analyst report sizes a "sports mental-performance app" market specifically. Adjacent estimates range from about $2B (sports-psychology services) to $22.7B (mental-health apps by 2030). They differ widely in scope and should be read only as direction, not as a TAM.

### Cited Findings
**Global / FIVB**
- The FIVB describes volleyball as the 4th most popular sport, with "more than 800 million fans", and has a stated goal of doubling to 1.6 billion by 2032. This comes from FIVB statements reported by Xinhua and People's Daily in Aug 2025. The often-quoted "800 million players" has no published FIVB methodology. One aggregator's regional table sums to 760M. — [People's Daily / Xinhua, Aug 2025](https://en.people.cn/n3/2025/0807/c90000-20350237.html); [SportsSurge (low-quality aggregator)](https://sportssurge.alibaba.com/volleyball/how-many-people-play-volleyball)
- Volleyball World's streaming service VBTV reached 1.5 million subscribers in September 2024. This shows a digitally reachable, paying volleyball audience exists. — [Volleyball World, About us](https://en.volleyballworld.com/about-us/about-volleyball-world)
- VNL 2024 recorded 127 million views on the Volleyball World website. This is viewership, not participation. — [Volleyball World, VNL 2024 recap](https://en.volleyballworld.com/volleyball/competitions/volleyball-nations-league/news/vnl-2024-concludes-with-record-breaking-global-viewership-and-attendance)
- The FIVB's "Empowering the Global Volleyball Movement" leaflets (V3 Sep 2025, V4 Dec 2025, V5 May 2026) report development progress, but the snippets contained no aggregate participation totals. — [FIVB V4 PDF](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FIVB-Empowering-the-Global-Volleyball-Movement-V4.pdf); [FIVB V5 PDF](https://www.fivb.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/FIVB-Empowering-the-Global-Volleyball-Movement-V5.pdf)

### Inferences
- The serviceable market for a volleyball-specific mental-skills product is the organized player base (club, school and college players plus their coaches), not the 800M figure. In the US that is on the order of 0.5M paying USAV members and 0.5M high-school girls, with heavy overlap. In Czechia it is about 58k registered members across about 1,500 clubs. A realistic early market is a few thousand to tens of thousands of players reached through clubs.
- The youth skew (Czech girls 3:1; US high-school girls the largest segment) means many users are minors. That shapes consent and payer (parent or club) decisions.
- The only measured market data is Sensor Tower's ($4.5B health and fitness in-app revenue, +13%). Category spending is growing, but downloads are flat, so new apps compete for a fixed pool of installs.

### Gaps
- There is no primary FIVB document defining "800 million players", and no current FIVB participation survey was found.
- No official ČVS annual-report membership breakdown for 2024-2026 was found (youth vs adult, indoor vs beach). The ~58k figure comes from a news citation.
- No reliable Czech or European beach volleyball participation trend data was found.
- No analyst report sizes "sports mental-performance apps" specifically. All figures here are adjacent-market proxies.

## 2. Monetization benchmarks: conversion, price, retention

### Takeaway
Freemium apps convert about 2% of users to paid (median). About 3-5% is "good", and hard-paywall apps reach about 12%. Health & Fitness median prices are about $9.70/month or $39.99/year, and users prefer annual plans. Retention is the main risk. Only about 30% of annual subscriptions survive to the second year, and only about 17% of new apps ever reach $1k MRR.

### Cited Findings
- Freemium median conversion is 2.18% versus 12.11% for hard-paywall apps (Adapty, vendor data). — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/markdown/freemium-to-premium-conversion-techniques.md)
- Freemium self-serve: 3-5% is "good" and 8-12% "great" (ChartMogul, SaaS). — [ChartMogul Conversion Report](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/). In Lenny's Newsletter survey data, about 33% of products convert 2.5-5% and 20% convert below 2.5%. — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/free-trial-conversion-guide)
- RevenueCat SOSA 2025 (75k+ apps, 2024 data): Health & Fitness median trial-to-paid is 39.9%, top decile 68.3%. The overall median download-to-trial start rate is 6.2% (top performers 20.3%). 82% of trials start on install day. H&F converts about 2x better on iOS than Android. — [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/?p=124212); [RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- Adapty (2026 report, 2025 data): Health & Fitness leads trial-to-paid at 35.0% but ranks last on first-renewal retention at 30.3% (as reported by a secondary summary). — [Adapty State of In-App Subscriptions 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2026)
- Trial length: 17-32-day trials have the highest median conversion at 45.7% (RevenueCat 2025). Adapty shows H&F at 48.8% for 17-32-day trials. — [RevenueCat trial insights](https://revenuecat.com/blog/growth/app-trial-conversion-rate-insights/); [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/?p=124212)
- Prices (RevenueCat SOSA 2025 via Airbridge): Health & Fitness medians are $9.70/month, $39.99/year and $4.99/week. The all-category monthly median is $6.68. Users strongly prefer annual plans in H&F. — [Airbridge 2026 pricing benchmarks](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/subscription-app-pricing-by-category-2026-benchmark)
- Adapty 2026 global medians (all categories, 2025): $7.48/week, $12.99/month, $38.42/year. — [Airbridge citing Adapty](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/subscription-app-pricing-by-category-2026-benchmark)
- Revenue per install (RevenueCat SOSA 2026 via secondary): H&F median is $0.48 at D14 and $0.66 at D60, the highest early monetization of any category. Download-to-paid within 35 days: 2.6% in North America (all categories). — [dev.to summary of SOSA 2026](https://dev.to/zaraai_0b75675ddc9204c716/ai-apps-generate-41-more-revenue-per-user-but-lose-79-of-annual-subscribers-before-month-13-485a)
- Retention: annual retention is 30.7% for non-AI apps versus 21.1% for AI apps (SOSA 2026, secondary). RevenueCat says about 56% of annual subscribers cancelled in year 1 in 2025, rising to about 72% in 2026. — [dev.to summary](https://dev.to/zaraai_0b75675ddc9204c716/ai-apps-generate-41-more-revenue-per-user-but-lose-79-of-annual-subscribers-before-month-13-485a); [RevenueCat 2026 benchmarks blog](https://revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026)
- Success distribution: 17% of new apps reached $1k MRR in 2025 (19% in 2024), and 4.6% reached $10k (5.3% in 2024) (SOSA 2026). Earlier coverage of SOSA 2025 said 17.2% reach $1k and 3.5% reach $10k within 2 years, with a median of 60 days to $1k. — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026 insights](https://www.revenuecat.com/sosa-26-insights); [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-apps-revenue-developers/)
- General app engagement: the median D30 retention across categories is about 4% (UXCam). Meditation-specific data is anecdotal: Insight Timer D30 16% (unverified blog), and Calm lost about 80% of users within the first 30 minutes (Iterable case). — [UXCam](https://uxcam.com/blog/mobile-app-retention-benchmarks.md); [Iterable](https://iterable.com/blog/consumer-lifestyle-apps-predict-silent-churn/)
- Competitor price points (App Store listings, may vary by country): NeuroFuel $12.99/month or $99.99/year (claims 1,000+ teams, 20k athletes). Restoic $7.99/month or $69.99/year. Mindbuddy $9.99-14.99/month, $69.99-99.99/year. Focus 5 $5/month plus 1:1 coaching. A sport-psychologist session costs about $150-300. — [NeuroFuel](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/neurofuel/id1527014617); [Restoic](https://apps.apple.com/qa/app/restoic-performance-mindset/id1557936632); [Mindbuddy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mindbuddy-performance/id6593676911?uo=4); [Focus 5](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1613789297)

### Inferences
- A reasonable base case for a free-core PWA with an optional premium tier is 2-4% of active users paying, at about €30-40/year. 8% is an upside case that would require strong club or coach push.
- Year-2 renewal of only about 30% means an annual plan must be won again every season. Volleyball's seasonal calendar (club season roughly Sep-May in Europe) supports a "season pass" framing.

### Gaps
- No volleyball-specific or sports-mental-skills-specific conversion or retention data was found.
- The primary RevenueCat pages could not be fetched, so category-level H&F download-to-paid and the exact EU price medians are unverified.
- No Headspace or Calm 2025 subscriber figures from reliable sources were found.

## 3. Monetization models that fit a niche sports app

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence-backed options for a solo founder with club ties are B2B or B2B2C: club or academy licenses with a coach dashboard. Comparable products charge about $500 to $2,500 per organization per year, or about $5-8 per athlete per year. Consumer freemium works best as a secondary layer. Sponsorship, affiliate and grants are supplements, not a business on their own.

### Cited Findings
**Team / club licenses and coach dashboards (B2B)**
- EXACT Sports Peak TEST: $500/year for an entire organization, with coach dashboard and unlimited athletes and teams. — [EXACT Sports](https://exactsports.com/map-v2-2026)
- My Mental Gym (vendor blog): $1,499-2,499/year per athletic department with coach dashboards. It estimates app-based mental training at about $5-8 per athlete per year, versus $5,000-25,000 per team per season for human consultants. This is the vendor's own claim. — [My Mental Gym](https://my-mental-gym.web.app/blog/mental-performance-training-cost-schools.html)
- Team-management benchmark: TeamSnap per-team plans cost $69.99-129.99/year, one team per plan, and club/league plans are reportedly "from $599/year" (Capterra, unverified against TeamSnap). — [TeamSnap help](https://helpme.teamsnap.com/article/1454-plan-faqs); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/123208/TeamSnap/pricing/); [SoftwareSuggest](https://www.softwaresuggest.com/teamsnap/pricing)
- NeuroFuel markets usage by "1,000+ teams", which suggests team distribution is a core channel for this category. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/neurofuel/id1527014617)

**Freemium consumer subscription**
- Benchmarks are in section 2: about 2% freemium median, H&F annual median $39.99. Pros: scalable and low-touch. Cons: low conversion, high churn, and only about 17% of apps reach $1k MRR. — see section 2 sources

**One-time purchase / paid courses**
- No benchmark data was found specific to one-time-purchase sports mental apps. Competitors offer 6-month or annual plans as quasi-season passes (NeuroFuel 6-month plan $64.99). — [NeuroFuel](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/neurofuel/id1527014617)

**Brand sponsorship (Mizuno, Mikasa, Molten)**
- Mizuno has been a Volleyball World global partner since 2022, extended in 2023 and 2025 through 2026. It frames the deal around "an ecosystem linking fans, athletes and partners through digital and live events". Mizuno is also USA Volleyball's apparel and footwear provider through 2028. — [Mizuno corporate, 2023](https://corp.mizuno.com/en/news/20230508); [Sportcal](https://www.sportcal.com/?p=142607); [USA Volleyball](https://usavolleyball.org/story/usa-volleyball-and-mizuno-to-partner-through-2028/)
- Mikasa supplies FIVB official balls (partnership to 2032) and AVC events in 2025-26. — [FIVB partners](https://www.fivb.com/inside-fivb/fivb/partners/); [AVC](https://asianvolleyball.net/new/?p=140940)
- No evidence was found of these brands sponsoring third-party training apps.

**Affiliate (books, gear)**
- Amazon US Associates pays 3% on Sports and Outdoors and 4.5% on physical books (undated official table; a third-party guide says Sports was cut from 4.5% to 3%). Rates differ by country: Canada lists Sports at 6%, and the UK lists Books and Outdoors at 4.5%. — [Amazon Associates compensation](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ/); [Lasso guide 2026](https://getlasso.co/amazon-affiliate-commission-rate/); [Amazon UK](https://affiliate-program.amazon.co.uk/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ)

**Grants / federation partnerships**
- Erasmus+ Sport Small-scale Partnerships (ERASMUS-SPORT-2026-SSCP) offer lump sums of €30,000 or €60,000 for 6-24-month projects. The 2026 deadline was 5 March 2026 (closed), and a 2027 call is listed. Applicants are typically organizations such as a club or academy, not individuals. — [EU AI call aggregator](https://www.euacc.ai/calls/ERASMUS-SPORT-2026-SSCP); [Erasmus+ programme guide](https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu/programme-guide/part-b/key-action-2/small-scale-partnerships)
- Czech NSA "Můj klub 2026" (call 8/2026) has an allocation of CZK 1.702 billion for clubs, with a reduction ratio of 0.88 applied (Feb 2026). It funds clubs, so a club could in principle budget an app license from its operating support. This is an inference, so check eligible costs. — [NSA call 8/2026 PDF](https://nsa.gov.cz/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/8_2026_Vyzva-MK26_web.pdf); [NSA payout news](https://nsa.gov.cz/nsa-zahajila-vydavani-rozhodnuti-a-vyplaceni-podpory-ceskeho-sportu-ve-vyzve-8-2026-muj-klub-2026/)

### Inferences
- Best fit for this founder: (1) a free player app as the funnel; (2) a paid club or academy license (coach dashboard, team aggregates of flow ratings, branded white-label option) priced around €300-600 per club per season, anchored to EXACT's $500 and TeamSnap's ~$599; (3) an optional player "Pro" at about €3-4/month or €29-39/year; (4) pilot funding through the founder's academy, an NSA-funded club budget, or an Erasmus+ partnership with clubs in other countries.
- Sponsorship is realistic mainly as local or regional deals (a Czech sporting-goods retailer, a club sponsor) or in-kind support, until the user base is in the tens of thousands.
- Affiliate income at 3-4.5% on gear or books is negligible at this scale. For example, €2,000 of referred sales at 4% is €80.

### Gaps
- No published pricing for volleyball-specific team mental-skills products, or for white-label licensing.
- No data on creator or influencer partnership economics for niche sports apps.
- No evidence was found on whether ČVS or CEV partner with third-party apps, or on their terms.

## 4. Practical requirements: payments, fees, VAT, GDPR/minors, medical-device risk

### Takeaway
A PWA can sell subscriptions via web checkout with no app-store commission. A merchant of record (Paddle or Lemon Squeezy, about 5% + $0.50) removes the EU VAT burden. Stripe is cheaper (about 2.9% + $0.30, plus about 0.5% for Stripe Tax) but leaves the founder as the seller. Czech users under 15 need parental consent. Mood or flow ratings may count as health data under GDPR Article 9. Avoid medical-purpose claims to stay outside the EU MDR.

### Cited Findings
- Payment fees: Paddle is 5% + $0.50 (merchant of record, handles VAT and chargebacks). Lemon Squeezy is 5% + 50¢ (Stripe-owned since 2024). One competitor blog says 7% + 50¢, and Rework says +1.5% on international sales. Stripe is 2.9% + $0.30 plus about 0.5% for Stripe Tax, with the founder remaining the seller. Stripe Managed Payments (its merchant-of-record product, 2026) costs 3.5% on top of standard Stripe fees. These are mostly 2026 blog sources, so verify against live pricing pages. — [dev.to comparison 2026](https://dev.to/jettfu/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-fee-comparison-2026-2c77); [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/tools/billing-revenue/paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy); [Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy/); [Creem](https://www.creem.io/blog/honest-payment-fee-breakdown-creem-vs-stripe-paddle-ls)
- App store fees, if the app is later wrapped natively: the Apple Small Business Program is 15% for developers under $1M, including subscriptions from the first payment. Google Play charges 15% on all auto-renewing subscriptions from day one. — [RevenueCat on Small Business Program](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/engineering/small-business-program); [MacRumors 2020](https://www.macrumors.com/2020/11/18/apple-drops-app-store-fees/)
- Apple in the EU: linking out to web checkout from a native app carries a 15% commission (10% reduced) under Apple's EU terms. RevenueCat says Apple's new EU terms take effect 1 Oct 2026. A PWA run in the browser is not an App Store app. That it falls outside IAP rules is an inference, not a source. — [RevenueCat EU web checkouts](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/company/apple-in-app-web-checkouts-eu); [Stripe guide](https://stripe.com/en-at/guides/how-app-developers-can-reduce-fees-and-create-an-optimized-checkout)
- Web vs in-app conversion: in RevenueCat's May 2025 single-app test, in-app purchase converted at least 30% better than web. Web checkout brought 6% fewer payers and about $0.93 per $1 of IAP revenue after fee savings. Trial-to-paid was 26.3% on web versus 25.0% in-app. RevenueCat sells web tooling. — [RevenueCat test](https://revenuecat.com/blog/growth/iap-vs-web-purchases-conversion-test); [Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/does-web-to-app-improve-profitability)
- EU VAT on digital services: there is a single EU-wide €10,000 threshold for cross-border B2C sales. Below it, the seller can charge home-country VAT (Czech standard rate). Above it, VAT is charged at the customer's country rate and can be filed through the One-Stop-Shop (OSS) with a single return. — [Irish Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/vat/vat-on-services/electronic-services/vat-and-electronically-supplied-services/index.aspx); [EC OSS explanatory notes](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2021-07/vatecommerceexplanatory_notes_28102020_en.pdf); [Your Europe](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/taxation/vat/cross-border-vat/index_ga.htm)
- GDPR digital consent age in Czechia: 15 (Act No. 110/2019 Coll., §7). Below 15, parental consent is needed where processing relies on consent for an information-society service. — [ÚOOÚ English translation of Act 110/2019](https://uoou.gov.cz/media/act-no-110-2019-coll.pdf); [Bird & Bird, Children in the Digital World: Czech Republic](https://childreninthedigitalworld.twobirds.com/home/czech-republic); [Baker McKenzie](https://resourcehub.bakermckenzie.com/en/resources/global-data-and-cyber-handbook/emea/czech-republic/topics/legal-bases-for-processing-of-personal-data)
- Health data: controllers need both an Art. 6 lawful basis and an Art. 9 exception. Explicit consent is the usual route for commercial wellbeing apps, and legitimate interests is not available for special-category data. Mental-wellness check-ins and mood data are treated as health data by several commentators. — [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2022/01/defining-and-using-health-data); [Mason Hayes & Curran](https://mhc.ie/latest/insights/gdpr-and-digital-health-is-your-software-fit-for-purpose); [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-is-special-category-data)
- Medical Device Regulation: under MDCG 2019-11, the manufacturer's intended purpose decides whether software is a medical device. Lifestyle and wellbeing apps without a medical purpose are not devices. Software that produces patient-specific outputs for a medical objective is more likely to be one. The recommendation is to document the "not a medical device" decision. — [EC MDCG 2019-11](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en); [Taylor Wessing on border issues](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/interface/2019/bodytech/border-issues-medical-devices-wellbeing-and-lifestyle-apps); [OpenRegulatory](https://openregulatory.com/mdcg/mdcg-2019-11)

### Inferences
- For a Czech solo founder, a merchant of record (Paddle or Lemon Squeezy) is the simplest way to sell to EU consumers: there is no OSS filing and refunds and chargebacks are handled. It costs about 2 percentage points more than Stripe. For B2B club invoices, a direct bank or Stripe invoice with reverse-charge VAT is likely cheaper. This last point needs an accountant to confirm.
- Product wording should stay in performance and wellbeing language ("focus", "routine", "reset", "flow"). Avoid "anxiety treatment", "diagnosis" or "therapy". Keep a one-page written MDR qualification rationale.
- Flow, mood and stress ratings tied to an identifiable athlete should be treated as potentially Art. 9 data. That means explicit consent, a parent flow for under-15s, data minimization (local-only by default fits the "optional accounts" design), and clear rules on what coaches can see. A DPIA is advisable if clubs view minors' data.

### Gaps
- EDPB or ÚOOÚ guidance specifically on sports-performance or "flow" ratings as health data was not found.
- Apple's exact EU terms effective October 2026 were not verified from Apple's own pages.
- Paddle and Lemon Squeezy fees were not verified against their live pricing pages.

## 5. Revenue scenario: 1,000 vs 10,000 active players

### Takeaway
Consumer subscriptions alone are small at this scale. At 10,000 active players with a 2-4% conversion rate at €39.99/year, revenue is roughly €6k-12k net per year. Club licenses add comparable or larger amounts from far fewer customers, so B2B should be the lead.

### Cited Findings (inputs)
- Conversion: 2.18% freemium median ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/markdown/freemium-to-premium-conversion-techniques.md)), 3-5% "good" ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)), 8% used as the "great" lower bound ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)).
- Price: €39.99/year, using the H&F annual median of $39.99 at near-parity ([Airbridge citing RevenueCat SOSA 2025](https://www.airbridge.io/en/blog/subscription-app-pricing-by-category-2026-benchmark)).
- Fees: merchant of record about 5% + €0.50 per transaction ([dev.to](https://dev.to/jettfu/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-fee-comparison-2026-2c77)). The price is assumed VAT-inclusive at the Czech 21% rate (assumption; the VAT rate varies by buyer country above the €10k threshold, see [Irish Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/vat/vat-on-services/electronic-services/vat-and-electronically-supplied-services/index.aspx)).
- Year-2 renewal is about 30% ([SOSA 2026 secondary](https://dev.to/zaraai_0b75675ddc9204c716/ai-apps-generate-41-more-revenue-per-user-but-lose-79-of-annual-subscribers-before-month-13-485a)).
- B2B anchors: $500 per organization per year ([EXACT Sports](https://exactsports.com/map-v2-2026)); $5-8 per athlete per year ([My Mental Gym](https://my-mental-gym.web.app/blog/mental-performance-training-cost-schools.html)); ČVS has about 1,511 clubs ([CZSO 2019](https://csu.gov.cz/plk/sport-v-plzenskem-kraji-v-roce-2019)).

### Inferences (arithmetic, illustrative only)
Net per annual subscriber: €39.99 − VAT (39.99 − 39.99/1.21 = €6.94) − MoR fee (5% × 39.99 + 0.50 = €2.50) ≈ **€30.55**.

| Active players | Conversion | Payers | Gross (€39.99/yr) | Net ≈ €30.55 each |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 2% | 20 | €800 | €611 |
| 1,000 | 4% | 40 | €1,600 | €1,222 |
| 1,000 | 8% | 80 | €3,199 | €2,444 |
| 10,000 | 2% | 200 | €7,998 | €6,110 |
| 10,000 | 4% | 400 | €15,996 | €12,220 |
| 10,000 | 8% | 800 | €31,992 | €24,440 |

- Renewal drag: if only about 30% renew, then in year 2 the 4%/10k cohort keeps about 120 of 400 payers. New sign-ups must replace about 280 each year to hold revenue flat.
- Club licenses (illustrative, using a €400/club/season assumption between EXACT's $500 and TeamSnap's ~$599): 10 clubs = €4,000/year; 50 clubs (about 3% of ČVS's ~1,511 clubs) = €20,000/year, before VAT and fees. Per-athlete pricing at €6/athlete/year × 2,000 athletes = €12,000.
- Takeaway: 50 paying clubs roughly match 10,000 active consumer users at 4% conversion. That is far fewer customers to acquire, and the founder has academy and club access.

### Gaps
- No volleyball-specific willingness-to-pay data. The €39.99 and €400 figures are benchmark-anchored assumptions, not validated prices.
- "Active players" is undefined in the benchmarks (monthly active vs installs). Conversion benchmarks are on downloads or free users, so the results above may be optimistic if many active users are casual.
- EU price elasticity: no EU-specific H&F price medians were retrieved.
