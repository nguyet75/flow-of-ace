# YouTube Content Landscape: Flow State, "The Zone" and Mental Performance for Athletes (Volleyball Focus)

Research date: 2026-11-24. About 27 web searches. Method note: YouTube pages could not be fetched directly, and several creator sites (flowresearchcollective.com, elitecompetitor.com, betteratbeach.com, thepricer.org, creatordb.app) were blocked by this session's network allowlist. Most figures below therefore come from **search-result snippets** of third-party stats pages (vidIQ, SocialCounts, SpeakRJ, Social Blade) or sales pages, and are marked as such. Treat subscriber and view counts as approximate, dated snapshots.

## 1. Biggest channels/videos on flow state and sports mental performance

### Takeaway
"Flow" content on YouTube is dominated by general peak-performance and neuroscience brands (Huberman Lab at ~8M subscribers; Steven Kotler / Flow Research Collective; Finding Mastery), not by sport-specific creators. The biggest volleyball channel, Coach Donny's Elevate Yourself (~0.9M subscribers), is skills/jump-training focused. ## 2. Techniques taught most, and evidence-backed vs hype

### Takeaway
The recurring toolkit is breathing (physiological sigh / cyclic sighing, box breathing), pre-performance routines, visualization/imagery, reframing nerves, controlling controllables, and Kotler-style "flow triggers" plus neurochemistry narratives; adjacent content pushes binaural beats/"flow music" and cold exposure. Routines, imagery and excitement reappraisal have solid meta-analytic or experimental support; cyclic sighing has one good RCT (mood/arousal, not sport performance); "flow triggers" are untested hypotheses; binaural beats are mixed; cold exposure is a recovery tool, not a pre-match mental tool.

### Cited Findings
- **Pre-performance routines (PPRs):** 2021 meta-analysis (Rupprecht, Tran & Gröpel; 112 effect sizes, ~800 athletes, 15 sports): small effect in pre-post designs (SMC = 0.31) and moderate-to-large effects in experimental designs under low pressure (g = 0.64) and pressure (g = 0.70); not moderated by routine type, age, gender or skill level — [Int Rev Sport Exerc Psychol](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/1750984X.2021.1944271), [Univ. Vienna press release](https://medienportal.univie.ac.at/media/aktuelle-pressemeldungen/detailansicht/artikel/athletes-with-a-pre-performance-routine-perform-better)
- **Imagery/visualization:** Simonsmeier et al. (2020) meta-analysis: overall d = 0.431 for imagery interventions; benefits for motor, motivational and affective outcomes; stronger effects with more intensive training — [Uni Bielefeld record](https://pub.uni-bielefeld.de/record/2943784). A 2025 multilevel meta-analysis (86 studies, 3,593 athletes) suggested ~10 min, 3x/week over ~100 days as the strongest dose — [Behavioral Sciences / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12109254/)
- **"I'm excited" reappraisal:** Brooks (2014, J Exp Psychol Gen) found saying "I am excited" vs "I am calm/anxious" improved karaoke, public-speaking and math performance (e.g., karaoke accuracy 81% vs 69% for "I am anxious") — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24364682/), [APA press release](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2013/12/performance-anxiety). A 2025 direct replication found higher excitement but not lower anxiety — [Reference Global replication PDF](https://reference-global.com/download/article/10.58734/plc-2025-0011.pdf)
- **Cyclic sighing / physiological sigh:** Balban et al. 2023 (Cell Reports Medicine; Huberman & Spiegel labs) RCT: 5 min/day for a month; cyclic sighing (prolonged exhales) produced the greatest mood improvement and respiratory-rate reduction, and breathwork beat mindfulness meditation on mood — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947), [Huberman Lab publication page](https://hubermanlab.stanford.edu/publications/brief-structured-respiration-practices-enhance-mood-and-reduce-physiological-arousal), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/1163028315). Note: outcomes were mood/arousal, not athletic performance.
- **Flow triggers:** Kotler's list began as "17 triggers" (The Rise of Superman) and later appeared as 22 in interviews — [EliteFTS](https://elitefts.com/education/17-triggers-to-induce-flow), [GreenBook](https://www.greenbook.org/insights/rethink13-hey-ho-lets-flow). A 2024 Theory & Psychology paper argues flow research faces a "crisis" in theoretical explanation — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09593543241237492); a review found flow operationalized 24 different ways across 42 studies — [PMC7033418](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7033418). A neuroscience review co-authored by Kotler describes "currently theorized 'flow triggers'" as preconditions still to be explained — [Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/693577596/First-Few-Seconds-for-Flow). No direct published test validating the trigger list was found.
- **Binaural beats / "flow music":** a 2022/2023 meta-analysis (15 studies) found a medium pooled effect on attention/memory (g = 0.40) but inconsistent results by frequency — [PubMed 35842538](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35842538/); a 2023 systematic review flagged heterogeneous methods — [PMC10198548](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10198548/); controlled studies of 40 Hz and 16 Hz beats found no attention benefit — [PMC10157589](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10157589), [PubMed 33978805](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33978805/)
- **Cold exposure:** evidence is mainly for recovery (power, soreness) after exercise, not for pre-competition mental state; a 2026 umbrella review (via EurekAlert) cautions CWI may be counterproductive before explosive movements without re-warming — [Sports Medicine 2022 meta-analysis](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s40279-022-01644-9), [EurekAlert 2026](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1152184), [PubMed 23434565](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23434565/)
- **Volleyball coaching sources** emphasize pre-serve routines, not lingering at the service line, deep breaths plus short self-talk cues, reframing nerves as normal, rehearsing pressure in practice and team visualization — [Peak Sports: serve misses](https://www.peaksports.com/sports-psychology-blog/dont-talk-yourself-into-service-miss-in-volleyball/), [Peak Sports: preparing for pressure](https://www.peaksports.com/sports-psychology-blog/how-to-prepare-for-pressure-in-volleyball/), [USA Volleyball: five keys to better serving](https://usavolleyball.org/resource/five-keys-to-better-serving/), [VolleyCountry](https://volleycountry.com/training/crush-your-nerves-and-play-your-best-every-game)
- Better at Beach's mindset course covers goal-setting and building routines — [Better at Beach offer](https://www.betteratbeach.com/mastering-the-volleyball-mind-1)

### Inferences
- Flow of Ace's core components (routine, imagery, "I'm excited" reframe, cue word, if-then plan, exhale-weighted breathing) map onto the best-supported techniques in this landscape, while it omits the weakest ones (triggers, neurochemistry talk, binaural beats, cold plunges). That is a defensible differentiator: "the evidence-based subset, made actionable."
- The app's breathing claims should stay modest: the cyclic-sighing RCT shows mood/arousal effects, not on-court performance.

### Gaps
- No content analysis was possible of which technique is mentioned most often across YouTube videos (no transcript/search-volume data); prevalence is inferred from sources found.
- If-then plans (implementation intentions) and cue words in sport were not separately searched in this pass.

### Takeaway

## 5. Gaps none of this content fills, and partnership/affiliate opportunities

### Takeaway
All the content found is passive (watch/listen) or course-based; none delivers a guided, timed, personalized pre-match routine, in-match micro-tools, or post-match feedback that learns a player's optimal arousal and breathing method. Volleyball creators with courses but no app (Better at Beach, Elite Competitor, Art of Coaching Volleyball, Elevate Yourself) are natural partners.

### Cited Findings
- Volleyball mental programs are self-paced courses/systems (Elite Mental Game; Better at Beach 5-part course; AOCV video library of 1,500+ videos) — [Elite Mental Game](https://elitecompetitor.com/emg), [Better at Beach](https://www.betteratbeach.com/mastering-the-volleyball-mind-1), [AOCV homepage](https://theartofcoachingvolleyball.com)
- Research supports routines and imagery under pressure, and imagery dose-response (frequent short sessions) favors repeated, guided practice that video does not enforce — [PPR meta-analysis](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/1750984X.2021.1944271), [Imagery meta-analysis 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12109254/)
- Flow research itself lacks validated interventions: a 2023 Frontiers review notes "a striking lack of empirical research on flow interventions" (work domain) — cited in search summary; see [PMC10360049](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10360049/)
- Better at Beach runs a partner page and a podcast featuring guests like Coach Donny, indicating openness to collaborations — [Better at Beach partner page](https://www.betteratbeach.com/partner-mastering), [Podcast ep. 73](https://zh.player.fm/series/get-better-at-beach-volleyball/episode-73-the-highs-and-lows-of-elevate-yourself-with-coach-donny)

### Inferences
- Gaps the app can claim: (1) "do it now" guided routine on the bench, timed to the match; (2) adaptive breathing/music tempo by energy check-in rather than one protocol for everyone; (3) in-match tools (serve routine, 3-second reset) that video cannot deliver; (4) personal data loop (flow ratings that learn best energy zone/breathing); (5) evidence-honest framing in a space full of "triggers" and neurochemistry claims; (6) free, with no sales funnel.
- Partnership ideas: co-branded routine packs or "cue word" sets with Better at Beach or Elite Competitor (their courses teach; the app executes); a free tool AOCV can recommend to coaches; Elevate Yourself shorts demonstrating the clutch-serve routine for reach; affiliate link-back from the app to creator courses for deeper learning (revenue share), avoiding upselling inside the athlete flow.
- Risk: Elite Competitor and similar sellers may see a free app as competition; frame it as a companion that drives course completion.

### Gaps
- No creator affiliate program terms were found (Better at Beach, Elite Competitor, AOCV, Elevate Yourself). No data on whether any volleyball creator already promotes a mental-training app.
- Flow of Ace's own site (https://nguyet75.github.io/flow-of-ace/) was not re-fetched in this pass; feature comparison relies on the brief provided.
