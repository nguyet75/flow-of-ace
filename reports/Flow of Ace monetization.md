# Charge the clubs, keep players free

Flow of Ace fits a real gap, and it can earn money, but not mainly from players. The pain points that coaches, sport psychologists and volleyball creators describe (nerves before a match, missed serves under pressure, spiralling after errors) are the ones its tools target: a timed pre-match routine, the clutch serve routine, the 3-second reset, the between-sets check-in, and post-match flow ratings that learn what works for each player. YouTube and creator content teaches the same evidence-backed core: routines, imagery, reappraising nerves as excitement, and long-exhale breathing. That content is passive or sold as a course, and **none of the products found delivers a guided, in-the-moment routine that adapts to the player**. The paid athlete apps are mostly audio and visualization libraries at about **$5–15/month or $45–130/year**. The one volleyball-specific rival, NeuroFuel, wins through clubs and associations, claiming **1,200+ teams** and a JVA partnership. Benchmarks say freemium consumer apps convert about **2%** of users, and only about **30%** of health and fitness annual subscribers renew. At 10,000 active players, a consumer tier alone would net roughly **€6k–12k a year**. Fifty paying clubs at about €400 a season would bring in about **€20k**. The recommendation is to keep the player app free, sell a **club/academy season licence with a coach dashboard (about €400 per club per season)** as the main product, and add an optional **Player Pro tier at about €29.99 a year** later. Sell through a merchant of record so EU VAT is handled, build for GDPR rules on minors and health data from day one, and pilot through the founder's own academy and club network in the 2026–27 season. One limit applies to everything below: **Reddit could not be reached from the research environment**, so no verified player quotes from Reddit appear here, and **most prices and statistics come from search-result snippets** rather than direct page reads.

> **Evidence caveats (read first).** (1) **Reddit was unreachable.** WebSearch restricted to reddit.com was refused ("not accessible to our user agent"), WebFetch to www.reddit.com was refused, mirror hosts were outside the session's allowed network, and about 20 general searches almost never returned Reddit threads. The only Reddit items in this report come from search snippets and are labelled as such. No Reddit quote was invented. Statements about what "players say" rest on coaches, sport-psychology practices, local press and podcast titles, not on Reddit. (2) **Most prices, ratings, subscriber counts and benchmark figures come from search snippets or secondary summaries.** App Store pages, most vendor sites, RevenueCat and YouTube were blocked for direct fetching. Treat every price as "seen in a snippet on 2026-11-24" until checked on the live page. (3) **The live app was not re-fetched** during this research. Its feature description comes from the project brief and the repository README. Evidence grades used below: **A** = measured or primary data; **B** = several consistent secondary or snippet sources; **C** = a single snippet, a vendor claim, or an assumption anchored to benchmarks; **D** = inference.

## Players' stated problems map onto every in-match tool, but Reddit could not confirm it

The core pain points are consistent across every non-Reddit source reached. A high-school player told local press that "the negative thoughts start coming out once I start making more errors" ([The Signal via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/the-signal/20181004/281745565330642)). A sport-psychology practice describes players who "serve consistently in training, then struggle to reproduce that contact when the match starts" ([Condor Performance](https://condorperformance.com/volleyball-sports-psychologist/)). A coaching site lists "fear of embarrassment after a shank" among serve-receive triggers ([SportsSteps](https://sportssteps.com/how-to-teach-kids-volleyball-serve-over-the-net/)). Podcast episodes from The Elite Competitor, a volleyball mental-training business, are built around fixing **"in-game spirals"** ([Raising Elite Competitors](https://www.buzzsprout.com/1720090/episodes/19245626-how-olivia-fixed-her-in-game-spirals-and-won)). USA Volleyball publishes several resources on athlete anxiety and pressure ([USA Volleyball](https://usavolleyball.org/resource/tips-for-helping-athletes-overcome-sports-anxiety/)). Each of these maps onto a Flow of Ace tool: pre-match nerves to the routine and breathing, the shanked pass and the error spiral to the 3-second reset, and set-point serving to the clutch serve routine.

The Reddit evidence is thin. Aggregator snippets put **r/volleyball at about 291K–295K members**, which makes it the largest volleyball community on Reddit ([Hive Index](https://thehiveindex.com/communities/r-volleyball/)). One snippet shows a 2022 user asking the sub's weekly short-questions thread how others deal with nerves before a match, with no replies visible ([r/volleyball via mirror](https://axebps-redlib.hf.space/r/volleyball/comments/v5yhpx/weekly_short_questions_thread/ic4ey47/?context=3)). If that pattern holds, nerves questions land in a low-visibility megathread rather than as standalone posts. r/sportspsychology appears in mirrors to lean toward practitioners and students rather than athletes ([mirror](https://reddit.rtrace.io/r/sportspsychology/)). **Whether these are the top complaints on Reddit, which techniques Redditors upvote, and whether players there say they would pay for an app are all unknown** (evidence grade D for any Reddit-specific claim).

Two findings should shape the product. First, a 2023 study of female volleyball players found **cognitive anxiety (worry, overthinking) higher than somatic anxiety (physical jitters)** ([pre-competitive anxiety study](https://oalib-perpustakaan.upi.edu/Record/doaj_1a426b62c0864c909742680148c8e9d6)). That makes the cue-word, process-goal and if-then steps at least as important as the breathing step. It is one study, so grade C. Second, a source on performance anxiety warns that **a one-off intervention just before a big competition rarely lasts** ([Success Starts Within](https://www.successstartswithin.com/sports-psychology-articles/sports-performance-anxiety/what-is-sports-performance-anxiety/)). The app therefore needs a practice-mode rehearsal habit, not only match-day use. There is also a sceptical coach view: one coaching blogger, asked for pressure-serving drills after two missed set-point serves, replied that "2 missed serves by two different players isn't a trend" ([Coaching Volleyball](https://coachingvb.com/creating-service-pressure-in-practice/)). A "clutch serve" feature will need to win over coaches who treat such misses as noise, and that matters if coaches are the buyers.

## YouTube teaches the right toolkit as passive video and paid funnels

Flow content on YouTube is dominated by generalist neuroscience and peak-performance brands, not by sport. **Huberman Lab has about 8M subscribers** ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@hubermanlab/), snippet). Steven Kotler and the Flow Research Collective reach their audience through Big Think videos and podcast appearances. One aggregator snippet put a Kotler Big Think flow video at about 222K views ([rosetta.to](https://rosetta.to/u/bigthink/slow-down-your-brain-to-get-more-done-with-steven-kotler-big-think)). The largest volleyball creator, **Coach Donny's Elevate Yourself, has about 0.9M subscribers** and focuses on skills and jump training ([SpeakRJ](https://www.speakrj.com/audit/report/UCoEMagRUvrXELuJZwS4DevA/youtube), snippet). Volleyball mental-game content comes mostly from small businesses that use it to sell courses: The Elite Competitor, Better at Beach and Peak Performance Sports ([Coach Her Game](https://coachhergame.buzzsprout.com/2455264/episodes/18240876-stop-hoping-for-mental-toughness-start-training-it-3-strategies), [Better at Beach](https://www.betteratbeach.com/mastering-the-volleyball-mind-1), [Peak Sports](https://www.peaksports.com/volleyball-mental-game-secrets/)). YouTube comments could not be retrieved, so viewer demand is inferred from episode titles and coaching Q&A (grade C).

The techniques taught split cleanly by evidence, and Flow of Ace already sits on the strong side. **Pre-performance routines** have meta-analytic support, with effects of g = 0.64 under low pressure and g = 0.70 under pressure in experimental designs ([Rupprecht et al. 2021](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/abs/10.1080/1750984X.2021.1944271)). **Imagery** shows an overall d = 0.43, and a 2025 meta-analysis found the strongest dose at about 10 minutes, three times a week ([Simonsmeier et al.](https://pub.uni-bielefeld.de/record/2943784); [PMC12109254](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12109254/)). **"I'm excited" reappraisal** improved performance in Brooks's experiments ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24364682/)), though a 2025 replication found more excitement but no drop in anxiety ([replication](https://reference-global.com/download/article/10.58734/plc-2025-0011.pdf)). Earlier verification work for this project also found the reappraisal effect falls from d = 0.23 to 0.14 after correcting for publication bias. **Cyclic sighing** beat mindfulness meditation on mood and respiratory rate in a one-month randomized trial, but it measured mood and arousal, not sport performance ([Balban et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947)). The popular extras are weaker. Kotler's "flow triggers" list grew from 17 to 22 items and has no published validation ([EliteFTS](https://elitefts.com/education/17-triggers-to-induce-flow)). A 2024 paper says flow theory faces a "crisis" of explanation ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09593543241237492)). Binaural-beat results are inconsistent ([PubMed 35842538](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35842538/)). Cold exposure is a recovery tool, and one review cautions it may hurt explosive movement if done before play without re-warming ([EurekAlert 2026](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1152184)).

The gap is delivery, not knowledge. Every volleyball mental product found is a self-paced course or video library: The Elite Competitor's "Elite Mental Game" lists at **$1,997** ([Elite Competitor](https://elitecompetitor.com/elite-mental-game-1), snippet), Better at Beach's 5-part mindset course costs **$97** ([Better at Beach](https://www.betteratbeach.com/offers/RVTbGiqW)), and The Art of Coaching Volleyball offers 1,500+ videos at **$12.99/month** ([AOCV](https://www.theartofcoachingvolleyball.com/join-us)). None of them runs a timed routine on the bench, delivers a reset between rallies, or learns a player's best arousal level from post-match ratings. There is also distrust of upsell funnels in the flow space. Trustpilot reviews of the Flow Research Collective include one titled "FRC Coaching Course was a Sleazy Cash Grab" ([Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/flowresearchcollective.com)). An app that is free, honest about its evidence and has no sales funnel is a credible position. The risk is that course sellers see a free app as a competitor. Pitching it as a companion ("their course teaches, the app executes") turns them into partners. Better at Beach already runs a partner page ([Better at Beach partners](https://www.betteratbeach.com/partner-mastering)).

## Fifty clubs out-earn ten thousand casual players

The reachable market is the organized player base, not volleyball's often-quoted "800 million", which FIVB statements frame as fans and which has no published method ([People's Daily/Xinhua](https://en.people.cn/n3/2025/0807/c90000-20350237.html)). USA Volleyball has **476,185 paying members** ([USOPC 2024 report](https://assets.contentstack.io/v3/assets/blt9e58afd92a18a0fc/blt6949650396c0735f/685b1f182e147f143f77ed35/USA_Volleyball_2024.pdf), grade A). US high-school girls' volleyball passed 500,000 participants in 2025–26 ([AVCA](https://www.avca.org/blog/girls-volleyball-reaches-historic-half-million-milestone/)). In the founder's home market, the Czech federation (ČVS) has **almost 58,000 registered members**, with registered youth girls outnumbering boys about 3:1 ([ČTK](https://www.ceskenoviny.cz/zpravy/2725661), grade B). ČVS had **1,511 clubs** at the end of 2019 ([CZSO](https://csu.gov.cz/plk/sport-v-plzenskem-kraji-v-roce-2019), grade A but dated). The category is growing in spending but not in installs: health and fitness in-app revenue rose about **13% to ~$4.5B in 2025** while downloads were flat ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/state-of-mobile-health-and-fitness-in-2025), grade A/B via secondary reporting). New apps are competing for a fixed pool of installs.

Consumer subscription maths is unforgiving. Freemium apps convert a median **2.18%** of users ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/markdown/freemium-to-premium-conversion-techniques.md)), and 3–5% counts as good ([ChartMogul](https://chartmogul.com/reports/saas-conversion-report/)). Health and fitness has the best trial-to-paid rate of any category but the **worst first renewal, about 30%** ([Adapty 2026](https://adapty.io/state-of-in-app-subscriptions-2026)). Only **17% of new apps reach $1k in monthly recurring revenue** ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/sosa-26-insights)). Team software is priced very differently. EXACT Sports charges **$500/year for a whole organization** with a coach dashboard ([EXACT Sports](https://exactsports.com/map-v2-2026)). TeamSnap's per-team plans cost $69.99–129.99/year ([TeamSnap](https://helpme.teamsnap.com/article/1454-plan-faqs)). A vendor estimates app-based mental training at **$5–8 per athlete per year**, against $5,000–25,000 per team per season for a human consultant ([My Mental Gym](https://my-mental-gym.web.app/blog/mental-performance-training-cost-schools.html), vendor claim, grade C). Evidence also points at coaches as the better buyer: a 2024 meta-analysis found coach-focused training reduced athlete anxiety (effect 0.37), while parent-focused training produced only one non-significant estimate ([sportspsychology.org summary](https://www.sportspsychology.org/articles/sports-parents-2026-09-25/)).

The revenue scenario below uses those benchmarks. All of its inputs are assumptions anchored to benchmarks, so the scenario as a whole is grade C. Net revenue per consumer subscriber subtracts Czech VAT at 21% (price VAT-inclusive) and a merchant-of-record fee of 5% + €0.50. At €39.99/year that leaves **€30.55**. At the recommended €29.99/year it leaves **€22.79**. Club licences are assumed at **€400 per club per season**, between EXACT's $500 and TeamSnap's club plan reported at about $599, and are shown before VAT and fees.

A realistic **year-one blend** (the 2026–27 season pilot plus first sales) is 10 clubs and 1,000 active players at 3% on Pro, giving about **€4,700–4,900**. That is enough to validate the model and pay for tooling, not to pay a salary. A **year-three target** of 50 clubs plus 10,000 active players at 4% gives about **€29k–32k**. Renewal is the swing factor. If only 30% of consumer annual subscribers renew, the 400-payer cohort shrinks to about 120 in year two, and about 280 new payers are needed each year just to stay level. Clubs renew on a season cycle and are fewer, known customers, so club churn is easier to manage. That is the main reason to lead with B2B. Two other channels matter less. **Affiliate income is negligible**: Amazon pays 3% on sports goods and 4.5% on books ([Amazon Associates](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/help/node/topic/GRXPHT8U84RAYDXZ/)), so €2,000 of referred sales earns about €80. **Brand sponsorship** from Mizuno or Mikasa is aimed at federations and events, and no case of them sponsoring a third-party training app was found ([Mizuno](https://corp.mizuno.com/en/news/20230508)). Local Czech sponsors are the realistic version.

## A Czech founder can sell from a PWA with little store tax, but minors' data sets the rules

**Payments.** Because Flow of Ace is a web app (PWA), it can take payments through web checkout and pay no app-store commission. A merchant of record such as Paddle or Lemon Squeezy (about **5% + $0.50**) becomes the legal seller, collects and files EU VAT, and handles chargebacks. Stripe costs about 2.9% + $0.30 plus roughly 0.5% for Stripe Tax, but leaves the founder as the seller ([fee comparison](https://dev.to/jettfu/stripe-vs-paddle-vs-lemon-squeezy-fee-comparison-2026-2c77), grade C; check live pricing). The **EU-wide €10,000 threshold** for cross-border sales of digital services to consumers decides where VAT is due: below it, the seller's home-country rules apply; above it, VAT is charged at each customer's country rate, usually through the One-Stop-Shop ([Irish Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/vat/vat-on-services/electronic-services/vat-and-electronically-supplied-services/index.aspx); [EC OSS notes](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2021-07/vatecommerceexplanatory_notes_28102020_en.pdf), grade A). Club licences are business sales, so a plain bank-transfer invoice is cheaper than any card processor. A Czech accountant should confirm three things the research did not cover: the trade-licence and income-tax setup, the VAT position of selling to Czech clubs (many are associations, not VAT payers), and whether the founder must register for Czech VAT.

**Store fees, if the app is wrapped natively later.** Apple's Small Business Program charges **15%** for developers under $1M a year ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/?page=2)). Apple's new EU terms take effect on 1 October 2026 and put a commission on link-out purchases, with rates that differ across Apple's own pages ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/company/apple-in-app-web-checkouts-eu); [Apple Newsroom](https://apple.com/newsroom/2026/08/apple-announces-changes-for-apps-in-the-european-union)). In the US, the Epic v. Apple anti-steering case is before the Supreme Court and no link-out rate has been set ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/330796/20261116/apple-supreme-court-contempt-epic-says-27-link-fee-defied-order-solicitor-general-disagrees.htm)). Google Play's settlement cuts subscription fees to about 10%, plus a separate billing fee ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/04/google-settles-with-epic-games-drops-its-play-store-commissions-to-20)). These rules are moving quickly (grade B/C). The PWA's real cost is friction on iPhone: there is no install prompt, and web push only works after the user adds the app to the Home Screen ([firt.dev](https://firt.dev/notes/pwa-ios/)). That hurts match-day reminders for teenage iPhone users. One data point argues against moving everything to the web: in a RevenueCat test, in-app purchase converted **at least 30% better** than web checkout ([RevenueCat](https://revenuecat.com/blog/growth/iap-vs-web-purchases-conversion-test), single-app vendor test, grade C).

**Data protection.** Czech law sets the GDPR **digital-consent age at 15** (Act 110/2019 Coll., §7). Below 15, parental consent is required where processing relies on consent ([ÚOOÚ translation](https://uoou.gov.cz/media/act-no-110-2019-coll.pdf), grade A). Mood, stress and flow ratings linked to an identifiable athlete are treated by several commentators as **health data under GDPR Article 9**. That requires explicit consent, because legitimate interest is not available for this kind of data ([Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2022/01/defining-and-using-health-data); [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-is-special-category-data)). No regulator guidance specific to sports or flow ratings was found. The current design, which stores data on the device by default and makes accounts optional, fits data minimization well. The paid product changes that: a coach dashboard that shows minors' ratings to club staff needs a parent consent flow, clear rules on what coaches can see (aggregates by default), a data-processing agreement with each club, and a data protection impact assessment.

**Medical-device risk.** Under the EU guidance MDCG 2019-11, the intended purpose stated by the maker decides whether software is a medical device, and wellbeing apps without a medical purpose fall outside the rules ([EC MDCG 2019-11](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en), grade A). Copy should stay with words like "focus", "routine", "reset" and "flow" and avoid "anxiety treatment", "therapy" or "diagnosis". The founder should keep a one-page written note explaining why the app is not a medical device.

## Recommendation: a club season licence, a free player core, and a 90-day pilot

| Decision | Recommendation | Evidence grade |
|---|---|---|
| **Model** | Free player app as the funnel; paid **club/academy season licence** with coach dashboard as the lead product; optional **Player Pro** as a second layer | B (pattern across NeuroFuel, EXACT, Hudl, benchmarks) |
| **Club price** | **€400 per club per season** (Sep–May), unlimited athletes; launch offer €200 or a free first season for 3–5 pilot clubs in exchange for usage data and a testimonial; per-team option around €79–99 per season for single coaches | C (anchored to EXACT $500, TeamSnap $70–130 per team and ~$599 per club; no volleyball willingness-to-pay data) |
| **Player Pro price** | **€3.99/month or €29.99/year**, framed as a season pass; below the $39.99 median because the core stays free and parents pay for teens; test €39.99 later | C |
| **What stays free forever** | Pre-match routine, breathing, clutch serve routine, 3-second reset, between-sets check-in, basic flow history | D (positioning choice; supported by funnel distrust in the category) |
| **Payments** | Merchant of record for consumer sales; bank-transfer invoices for clubs; defer native in-app purchase until a wrapper ships | B/C |
| **Funding supplements** | Ask pilot clubs whether NSA "Můj klub 2026" operating support can cover a licence ([NSA call 8/2026](https://nsa.gov.cz/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/8_2026_Vyzva-MK26_web.pdf)); prepare an Erasmus+ Sport small-scale partnership (€30k/€60k lump sums, applicant must be an organization such as a club; the 2026 deadline has passed and a 2027 call is listed) ([Erasmus+](https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu/programme-guide/part-b/key-action-2/small-scale-partnerships)) | C (eligibility of app licences unverified) |

**What to build next, in order.** (1) A **coach/club dashboard** that shows team-level aggregates of routine completion and flow ratings, plus a team join code. This is the paid product, and its absence is Flow of Ace's biggest gap against NeuroFuel. (2) A **consent and privacy layer** to go with it: an age gate, a parent-consent flow for under-15s, explicit Article 9 consent, coach visibility set to aggregates by default, and a privacy notice and data-processing agreement for clubs. (3) A **practice mode** that rehearses the routine and imagery in training about three times a week. This follows the imagery dose findings and the warning that one-off pre-match interventions fade, and it gives clubs a reason to use the app outside match days. (4) **Guided voice audio** in Czech and English for the routine and reset. Voice is what competitors charge for, and it is the natural Pro content. (5) A **thin native wrapper** (for example Capacitor) only after the pilot proves retention, to get store discovery, ratings and reliable push on iPhone. Before charging, act on the earlier research for this project: retire or demote the generic screen-based visual drills that the README still lists. Multiple object tracking did not transfer to volleyball blocking in elite players, and an "evidence-honest" pitch to coaches fails if the app contains drills its own research rejects.

**First steps for the next 90 days.** Run a free pilot in the founder's academy and 3–5 Czech clubs through the second half of the 2026–27 season. Track weekly active players, routine completions per match, practice-mode use, and coach dashboard logins, and ask each club's decision-maker for a price reaction at €200 and €400. Write the privacy notice, the club data-processing agreement, a short impact assessment and the one-page medical-device note *before* any coach can see a minor's data. Set up a merchant-of-record account but keep Pro switched off until club demand is confirmed. Approach one creator partner, such as Better at Beach through its partner page or Elevate Yourself for short demo clips, offering a co-branded cue-word or routine pack that presents the app as a companion to their course. Finally, close the evidence gap this research could not: from a network where Reddit is reachable, read r/volleyball's self-promotion rules and collect real threads on nerves, serving and apps, and manually review comments on the top Elevate Yourself and Huberman breathing videos. Use what you find to adjust onboarding copy and the Pro feature set.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "will players pay for flow?" to "will coaches pay for evidence that their players are doing the work?" The consumer tier sits in a crowded market where most apps are content libraries priced at about $40–130 a year, and only about a third of annual subscribers come back. A free, volleyball-specific routine engine is a strong way to acquire players and a weak product to sell to them. The club licence is the opposite. Few customers, seasonal renewals and existing club budgets suit a solo founder who already has academy and club contacts, and NeuroFuel's 1,200-team footprint shows the channel works in this sport.

The biggest risks are not competitors. They are unverified demand and data obligations. No Reddit voice, no volleyball willingness-to-pay figure and no live price page backs the numbers here. The moment a coach can see a 14-year-old's flow ratings, GDPR's rules on minors and health data apply. A well-run pilot season that measures use and asks for prices will produce better evidence than any of these snippets. Building the consent layer alongside the dashboard is what will make clubs comfortable buying.
